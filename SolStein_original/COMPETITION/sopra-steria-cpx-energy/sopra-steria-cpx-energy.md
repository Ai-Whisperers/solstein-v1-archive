## Product Offering

cpX.Energy is a modular cloud platform serving multiple energy market roles (traders, DSOs, TSOs, storage operators, market area managers):

- **Billing (cpX.Billing)**: End-to-end energy billing and invoicing
- **Sales**: Customer and contract management for energy suppliers
- **Market Communication (MACO)**: GeLi, GPKE, MPES, GaBi process handling (German market comms)
- **Balancing Group Management**: Balancing group accounting and management
- **Metering Data Management (GDM)**: Smart meter data processing and management
- **Grid Operations**: DSO-specific operations support
- **Energy Data Management**: Time series and energy determination automation
- **Accounting**: Financial accounting integration

SaaS model with usage-based pricing allows smaller energy companies to participate.

### Threat Assessment vs Eneve

**Direct Overlap Areas**:
- **Balancing group management**: Both platforms handle balancing group accounting, GABi Gas processes, and weather data/load profile processing. cpX.Energy's EnBW implementation demonstrates scale for this exact capability.
- **Metering data management / Time series**: cpX.GDM directly competes with Eneve's time series management for smart meter data, energy determination, and aggregation. Both handle plausibility checks and anomaly detection.
- **Market communication**: cpX.MACO handles German GeLi/GPKE/MPES/GaBi processes. Critically, cpX.MACO also integrates with EDSN for Dutch smart meter retrieval -- this directly overlaps with Eneve's EDSN protocol capabilities.
- **Billing and settlement**: cpX.Sales and cpX.Billing provide end-to-end energy billing. Eneve's settlement module is a direct competitor in this space.
- **Energy data management**: Both platforms process time series, load profiles, and aggregated energy data for various market roles.

**Where Competitor is Stronger**:
- **Cloud-native SaaS**: cpX.Energy is born cloud-native with usage-based pricing, lowering the barrier for smaller utilities. Eneve's on-premise MSSQL model requires more upfront investment and infrastructure.
- **Hydrogen readiness**: cpX.Energy launched a dedicated hydrogen module (Jan 2026) with H2-Ready metering already in production -- billing gas-hydrogen mixtures is a regulatory requirement coming soon. Eneve has no visible hydrogen capability.
- **Parent company resources**: EUR 5.8B revenue parent with EUR 432M free cash flow, 51,000 employees, and a dedicated Head of AI on the German executive committee provides massive R&D investment capability that dwarfs Eneve's resources.
- **Multi-market-role platform**: cpX.Energy serves traders, DSOs, TSOs, storage operators, and market area managers from a single platform. Eneve is primarily focused on energy suppliers/traders and DSOs.
- **Mobile capabilities**: Event Monitor app (Dec 2025) provides real-time operational monitoring on mobile devices -- a modern UX advantage.
- **German market depth**: Serving EnBW (5.5M end customers), VNG, and major utilities gives cpX.Energy deep German market references that Eneve cannot match.

**Where Eneve is Stronger**:
- **Dutch market dominance**: Eneve is the established market leader in the Netherlands with deep EDSN integration, NL-specific protocol expertise, and long-standing Dutch utility relationships. cpX.Energy's NL presence appears limited to EDSN smart meter retrieval, not full Dutch market operations.
- **NL regulatory expertise**: Deep understanding of Dutch energy market regulations, allocation processes, and specific NL balancing rules that cpX.Energy has not demonstrated.
- **Nominations/Scheduling depth**: Eneve has mature TSO/DSO communication and EDSN nomination capabilities built over years. cpX.Energy's nomination support is primarily German-market focused.
- **Proven NL deployment base**: Existing Dutch customer base and operational track record that cannot be replicated quickly by a new entrant.
- **Migration to modern stack**: Eneve's active C#/.NET migration positions it for cloud deployment while maintaining on-premise support -- a flexibility some Dutch utilities still require.

**NL Market Entry Likelihood**: **Medium-High** - CRITICAL UPDATE from identification phase. cpX.MACO already integrates with EDSN for Dutch smart meter data retrieval. Sopra Steria acquired Ordina (NL-based IT services, Oct 2023) and has Sopra Steria NL operations (soprasteria.nl published VNG success story). The building blocks for a Dutch market push are in place: EDSN connectivity, local Sopra Steria NL presence, cloud-native platform, and usage-based pricing attractive to Dutch mid-market utilities. The main barrier is full NL regulatory protocol support beyond basic EDSN retrieval.

**Capability Expansion Likelihood**: **High** - The Hydrogen module (Jan 2026) and MPES addition (Apr 2024) demonstrate continuous capability expansion. Platform architecture is modular, allowing rapid addition of new market protocols. The move into hydrogen positions cpX.Energy ahead of most competitors for the energy transition. With Sopra Steria's restructuring around industry verticals (Oct 2024), energy is a strategic focus area.

**Strategic Implications**:
Sopra Steria cpX.Energy is Eneve's most structurally similar competitor -- both focus on energy back-office, balancing, metering, and market communication rather than front-office trading. The confirmed EDSN integration and Sopra Steria NL presence elevate the threat level significantly above the initial identification assessment. Eneve should monitor cpX.Energy's Dutch-language marketing, NL-specific protocol development, and any Dutch utility customer announcements. The strongest defensive strategy is to accelerate Eneve's own cloud/SaaS offering and AI capabilities, as cpX.Energy's cloud-native model with cpX.AI and hydrogen readiness represents the direction the market is moving. Eneve's deep NL regulatory expertise and existing customer base provide a window of advantage, but that window narrows as cpX.Energy adds NL market capabilities.
