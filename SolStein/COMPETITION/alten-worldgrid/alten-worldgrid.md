# ALTEN Worldgrid

> **Former names**: Atos Worldgrid (until Dec 2024), Worldgrid (division name retained post-acquisition)

## Identification

- **Company**: ALTEN Worldgrid (acquired by ALTEN SA from Atos SE, December 2024)
- **Product(s)**: Custom energy consulting, engineering, and software solutions
- **HQ Country**: France
- **Countries of Operation**: Europe-wide
- **Founded**: Originally part of Atos SE. Sold December 2024
- **Employees**: ~1,100 (at time of sale)
- **Revenue / Market Cap**: EUR 170M revenue (2023). Sold for EUR 270M enterprise value
- **Ownership**: ALTEN SA (France, public: Euronext Paris: ATE)
- **Website**: Previously atos.net (transitioning to ALTEN)

## Competitive Profile

- **Tier**: 3 - Adjacent / Infrastructure
- **Specialization**: Energy consulting, engineering services, IT/OT integration, digital transformation for utilities
- **Commodities**: Power, Gas (via client projects)
- **Platform**: Custom solutions / system integration
- **Cloud / On-Premise**: Both (project-dependent)

## Product Offering

ALTEN Worldgrid provides consulting and engineering services rather than a packaged product:

- **Digital Transformation**: Helping energy/utility companies modernize IT systems
- **IT/OT Integration**: Bridging operational technology with information technology
- **Custom Software Development**: Bespoke energy software solutions
- **System Integration**: Connecting disparate energy systems and platforms
- **Back-Office Operations**: Automating business processes for energy companies
- **Smart Grid Solutions**: Grid modernization and optimization

### Threat Assessment vs Eneve

**Direct Overlap Areas**:
- **System Integration**: ALTEN Worldgrid integrates energy IT systems at utilities, which could include recommending or implementing eBase alternatives at client sites. However, they do not build competing products.
- **Back-Office Automation**: Offers enterprise automation and back-office process optimization for utilities, but as bespoke project work rather than packaged software competing with eBase.

**Where Competitor is Stronger**:
- **Nuclear expertise**: 45 years of nuclear I&C, safety-critical SCADA systems, EPR2 programme -- entirely outside Eneve's domain
- **Scale of parent**: ALTEN's ~57,000 employees and ~30-country presence dwarfs Eneve; can bring massive engineering capacity to any energy client
- **Multi-commodity scope**: Covers nuclear, water, oil & gas, renewables -- broader than Eneve's electricity/gas focus
- **Client relationships**: Direct access to major European utilities (EDF, and likely others) through long-term nuclear/infrastructure contracts

**Where Eneve is Stronger**:
- **Product vs. services**: Eneve has a packaged, configurable software product (eBase); ALTEN Worldgrid only delivers bespoke solutions. Product approach is more scalable and lower total cost of ownership for clients
- **Energy market expertise**: Deep specialization in Dutch energy market operations (balancing, settlement, nominations, EDSN protocols) -- zero capability here
- **Time series & smart meter data management**: Purpose-built platform vs. custom one-off solutions
- **Market protocol compliance**: EDSN, TSO communication, EDI messaging -- completely outside scope
- **Speed to value**: eBase is deployable software; ALTEN Worldgrid projects take years

**NL Market Entry Likelihood**: Low - Worldgrid operates in FR/ES/DE only. While ALTEN parent has NL offices with an energy division, the Worldgrid unit's nuclear/infrastructure focus is far removed from Dutch energy back-office software. ALTEN NL does general engineering consulting, not energy market software.

**Capability Expansion Likelihood**: Low - Doubling down on nuclear (EPR2 is a 20-year anchor contract). No signals of moving into energy trading, balancing, or market operations software. The acquisition by ALTEN reinforces the engineering/consulting direction, not a product pivot.

**Strategic Implications**:
ALTEN Worldgrid is not a competitive threat to Eneve's core product business. The primary risk is as a **system integrator** that could influence procurement decisions at large utilities -- recommending competitors' products over eBase during digital transformation projects. However, this risk is mitigated by geographic distance from the NL market and lack of energy market protocol expertise. Monitor ALTEN NL's energy division for any signs of expanding into Dutch energy back-office consulting. The acquisition confirms the strategic value of energy sector expertise (EUR 270M price tag for 1,100 employees = ~EUR 245K/employee), useful as a talent market benchmark.
