# Solstein -- Why Now

## The Iceberg: A Day to Build, 2 Years to Be Able To

When a co-founder of a European energy software company -- a technical person who built a platform from scratch -- was asked how long it took to build Solstein, he said: **"2 years of learning AI."**

He knows the system was built in a day. But he also knows that day is only possible because of 2 years of daily AI practice -- understanding what models can and cannot do, learning how to structure prompts that produce consistent research-grade output, developing the judgment to know when AI is hallucinating versus delivering real intelligence, and building the frameworks that make results comparable across dozens of companies.

**That is what buyers are paying for.** Not the text files. The expertise baked into them.

Anyone can ask an AI model to "research a competitor." The result will be shallow, inconsistent, and unreliable. Solstein produces structured, scoreable, multi-dimensional intelligence with confidence-tagged data points -- because the person who built it spent 2 years learning exactly how to make AI do that.

## The Timing Advantage

- **Today**: The expertise gap is wide. Very few people know how to make AI produce reliable, structured competitive intelligence. Buyers pay for the skill that took 2 years to develop.
- **12-18 months**: More practitioners emerge. The AI models improve. The skill gap narrows but the quality gap persists -- because quality comes from domain judgment, not just prompt engineering.
- **24+ months**: The tooling commoditizes. The advantage shifts from *having the skill* to *having the client relationships and the track record*.

**The play**: Sell the expertise now, while few can match the quality. Lock in client relationships. Build the reference cases. By the time others catch up on the AI side, Solstein owns the market trust.

Everyone can ask AI a question. Very few can make it produce intelligence you'd bet millions on. That's the difference.

## The VC Problem Is Universal

Every PE/VC firm faces the same structural challenge:

- The PE firm doesn't have the AI expertise to assess the impact on their portfolio
- Traditional consulting is too slow (months), too expensive (EUR 500K+), and too static (a PDF that's stale on delivery)
- They don't even know the right questions to ask

Solstein answers the questions they don't know how to ask yet.
