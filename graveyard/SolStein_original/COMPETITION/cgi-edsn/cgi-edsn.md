## Product Offering

CGI's energy relevance in the Netherlands comes primarily through the EDSN partnership:

- **C-ARM Platform**: Central system processing meter data for all seven Dutch regional network operators
  - Daily and monthly settlement for electricity and gas
  - 15+ million metering points
  - Peak processing: 4 million meter data messages per day
  - 15-minute resolution for electricity, hourly for gas
- **EDSN Data Hub**: Central market facilitation platform for Dutch energy market
- **Market Participant Registry**: Registration and management infrastructure
- **Energy Consulting**: Broader IT consulting for energy utilities

## Notes

- Not a direct product competitor; CGI operates the market-level infrastructure that Eneve's clients interact with
- C-ARM is the system eBase sends/receives data from in the Dutch market
- Understanding CGI/EDSN's roadmap is critical: changes to C-ARM directly impact Eneve
- 15M+ metering points at 15-min resolution = massive data volumes that drive eBase requirements
- CGI's global scale (90k employees, CAD 14.3B revenue) dwarfs most energy software vendors
- Relationship is more ecosystem partner than competitor
- Risk: if CGI/EDSN expands C-ARM capabilities downstream toward participant-level operations
- Technolution Spark is another EDSN technology partner (built CERES registration system)

### Threat Assessment vs Eneve

**Direct Overlap Areas**:
- **Smart Meter Data Processing**: C-ARM processes the same 15M+ metering points that Eneve's eBase handles at the participant level. C-ARM is the market-level aggregator; eBase is the participant-level processor. They are complementary layers, not competitors -- but the boundary could shift.
- **Settlement & Allocation**: C-ARM performs daily/monthly settlement at market level; eBase performs settlement at the energy company level. Different scope but adjacent functionality.
- **Market Communication**: EDSN defines the protocols and message formats that eBase must implement. Changes to EDSN protocols directly drive eBase development work.

**Where CGI/EDSN is Stronger**:
- **Scale**: 94,000 employees, CAD 15.91B revenue, $1B AI investment dwarfs Eneve and all energy-specific competitors
- **Market Position**: 100% monopoly on Dutch energy market infrastructure -- every market participant depends on C-ARM
- **Multi-Country Reach**: Datahubs in 10 countries (NL, DK, FI, PL, NO, and more) -- can replicate patterns across markets
- **Investment Capacity**: Record cash generation (CAD 871.9M in Q1 alone), aggressive acquisition pace (6+ in 2024-2025)
- **AI Resources**: $1B AI investment, OpenAI partnership, PulseAI platform with 35+ pre-trained models
- **Cloud Infrastructure**: AWS-native with Kafka/Kappa architecture; proven at national scale

**Where Eneve is Stronger**:
- **Participant-Level Depth**: eBase operates at the energy company level with deep balancing, nominations, scheduling, and portfolio management -- capabilities C-ARM does not provide
- **Domain Specificity**: Eneve's entire business is energy back-office; CGI's energy division is a fraction of a massive IT conglomerate
- **Protocol Implementation**: eBase implements the participant-side of EDSN protocols (sending/receiving nominations, schedules, meter data); C-ARM implements the hub side. Eneve has deeper participant-workflow expertise.
- **Speed & Agility**: Small company can adapt faster to market changes than a 94,000-person organization
- **Customer Intimacy**: Direct, deep relationships with Dutch energy companies vs. CGI's infrastructure-level relationship with EDSN

**NL Market Entry Likelihood**: Low (direct competition) / High (ecosystem expansion) - CGI already operates the Dutch market infrastructure. The risk is not CGI entering Eneve's market as a new competitor, but rather EDSN/CGI expanding C-ARM capabilities downstream toward participant-level operations. If C-ARM adds participant-level settlement, portfolio management, or scheduling features, it could absorb functionality that energy companies currently rely on eBase to provide. The energy transition (more prosumers, energy communities, real-time data needs) creates pressure for more centralized functionality.

**Capability Expansion Likelihood**: Medium - CGI's Finland Datahub 2.0 already added energy community support and 15-minute metering. Poland's CSIRE is being built from scratch with modern capabilities. Each new datahub pushes the boundary of what market infrastructure provides. However, CGI's business model is infrastructure operation, not participant software -- expanding downstream would mean competing with their own clients' tool vendors, which is a significant strategic tension.

**Strategic Implications**:
CGI/EDSN is not a product competitor but represents the most strategically important entity in Eneve's ecosystem. Every protocol change, data format update, or capability expansion at EDSN directly impacts eBase development priorities. The $1B AI investment and OpenAI partnership signal that CGI will embed AI into its energy platforms -- if predictive/AI features appear in C-ARM (e.g., AI-powered allocation, anomaly detection in meter data, predictive settlement), it could reduce the value that participant-level tools like eBase provide. Eneve should monitor EDSN's roadmap closely, maintain strong relationships with EDSN technical teams, and ensure eBase remains the indispensable participant-level complement to C-ARM rather than a layer that C-ARM could absorb.
